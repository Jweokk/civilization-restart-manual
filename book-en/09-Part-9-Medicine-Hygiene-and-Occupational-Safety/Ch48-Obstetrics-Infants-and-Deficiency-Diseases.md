## Capability Boundary

**Can (this chapter gives the method and criteria):** clean delivery from start to finish; recognition of postpartum hemorrhage with uterine massage and abdominal compression; observation time limits and boundary-procedure declaration for retained placenta; recognition of puerperal fever and back-tracing of cleanliness breakpoints; newborn warming, cord cutting, and stump care; the breastfeeding system and the second-best substitute-milk plan; maternal nutrition and danger-signal recognition (when to stop work and take to bed); infant weight-gain monitoring and supplementary-food timing; prevention and recognition of the three major deficiency diseases — scurvy, iodine deficiency, and anemia; distinguishing protein-energy deficiency and vitamin A deficiency.

## 48.1 Population Is the Trump Card of a Civilization Restart

### 48.1.1 Maternal Death: The Leading Cause of Death Among People of Reproductive Age

This is not rhetoric; it is historical fact: in populations without obstetric care, **the risk of maternal death per birth is roughly 0.5%–1.5% (range in the historical records, varying widely across eras and regions), and a woman of reproductive age bearing 5–8 children faces a 3%–10% lifetime probability of dying from pregnancy and childbirth**; among deaths of women aged 15–40, maternity-related causes long held first place, above any single disease and above occupational injury. This route has no transfusion, no cesarean section, no anti-infection drugs — **this range will not disappear automatically because birth attendants exist; it will only be pressed toward the lower edge of the range by cleanliness, nutrition, and not intervening too early**. Every maternal death loses two assets at once: an adult woman in whom fifteen or more years of rearing and education have already been invested, and a potential artisan twenty years from now. For the Chapter 37 balancing table, this is the most expensive negative entry.

### 48.1.2 Generation-Turnover Time Versus the Skill-Transmission Cycle

Overlay the two tables of Chapter 37 and Chapter 43 and you get a constraint that is often ignored:

| Quantity | Value (empirical) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| From birth to full laborer | About 15 years | Chapter 37 inventory scope |
| From entering apprenticeship to level-3 (independent) artisan | A further 5–8 years | Chapter 43 four-level system |
| Formation cycle of one level-4 (can teach apprentices) artisan | About 15–20 years | Chapter 43, 43.6 |
| Doubling time at 1% annual population growth | About 70 years | 70 ÷ annual growth rate |
| Doubling time at 2% annual population growth | About 35 years | Same as above |
| Doubling time at 3% annual population growth | About 23 years | Same as above |

The inference is one sentence: **the skill-transmission cycle (15–20 years to produce one level-4 artisan) is shorter than the population generation-turnover time (20–70 years), so the apprentice supply of the Chapter 43 master–apprentice system depends entirely on the existing birth cohorts — one peak of infant deaths, or a single maternal death, becomes an artisan gap fifteen to twenty years later, and later still a stepwise regression of technology.** The Chapter 37 37.3.2 tier cross-reference table (how many people are needed to support reaching which chapter) assumes the population structure does not fall off a cliff; maintaining that "no cliff" is this chapter's place in the whole book. Iron Law Seven says labor is the first asset; this chapter pushes it to the extreme — **every safe birth is an investment in a twenty-year plan**, and its compounding beats any kiln.

### 48.1.3 This Chapter's Leverage Points

Ordered by avoidability, the top three levers on maternal death and infant death all lie outside "the moment of birth": **cleanliness (puerperal fever, 48.3/48.4), warmth (newborn hypothermia, 48.3/48.5), and breastfeeding with weight-gain monitoring (early detection of infant decline, 48.4/48.5)**. The review opinion states this explicitly: "attending only to the moment of birth while ignoring postpartum infection, newborn warming, and long-term nutrition" is the typical failure mode of this field. This chapter's space allocation follows that ordering: one section on delivery; postpartum and infants take two sections.

## 48.2 Pregnancy Management: Prevention Over Cure

Pregnancy management has one goal: **make the birth happen inside a settlement whose nutrition is adequate, whose danger signals have been identified, and whose delivery kit is ready**. To that end, establish the pregnancy and birth register: date of the last menstrual period, expected delivery month (the 10th solar month counted from the month of the last period, empirical ±0.5 month), danger signals checked month by month, delivery arrangements fixed 1 month prenatal.

### 48.2.3 The Four Preparations Before Delivery

Complete item by item 1 month prenatal (matching the 10 pre-start verification items; here, the human-side preparations):

1. **The birth attendant washes hands and nails**: nails trimmed to not extend past the fingertips, nail grooves scrubbed clean with soap; at delivery, follow the Chapter 45 three-level handwashing procedure (soap scrub from fingertips to elbows ≥2 rounds + rinse with cooled boiled water + alcohol rub; criterion: alcohol ≥50% can be lit).
2. **Boil the instruments**: scissors, ligature thread, and perineal pad cloths go into the pot when labor begins; soak ≥15 min at a rolling boil, lift out gripping with boiled cloths, and place into a boiled cloth bundle ready for use.
3. **Clean cloth pads**: two layers of large buttock cloth spread on the delivery platform (wooden plank bed + waterproof oilcloth or double-layer thick cloth); the bottom layer is not changed again after the mother's hip position is finally adjusted.
4. **Warm room**: light the fire half a day in advance to preheat the delivery room to the item-5 verification criterion; keep the infant-wiping cloths and swaddles in a warm place.

## 48.3 Clean-Delivery Procedure

This section is the core of the chapter. The historical lesson comes first: **the root of the puerperal-fever pandemics was not "childbirth itself being dirty," but attendants carrying filth from autopsies, fecal contamination, and wounds into the birth canal**. In the era before Semmelweis, women delivered by doctors died of puerperal fever at higher rates than those delivered in the street — **excessive intervention + contaminated hands = a death amplifier**. The three life-or-death pillars of this procedure are therefore: the three cleans, following the rhythm, and no intervention without indication.

### 48.3.1 The Three Cleans

| Clean target | Method | Criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Hands | Before delivery, soap wash + boiled-water rinse + alcohol rub per the Chapter 45 procedure; **one wash before and one after every internal examination during labor**; after touching anything outside the perineal field (doors, basins, infant clothing), must re-wash | Nails short, no visible soiling on the hands, stinging after the alcohol rub (skin intact) |
| Instruments | Scissors and thread boiled ≥15 min; after boiling, set them down only on boiled cloth and touch them only by the handle end | Instruments never rest on a non-boiled surface at any point |
| Delivery platform and the area around the birth canal | Double-layer buttock cloth; perineum rinsed front-to-back with cooled boiled clean water (back-to-front, which carries fecal contamination, is forbidden); perineal pad re-placed after the mother urinates or defecates | A soiled perineal pad is replaced immediately; no reuse |

### 48.3.2 Normal Labor Observation (No Hurrying, No Pulling)

| Labor stage | Observation | Normal range (empirical) | Crossing the line = prolonged labor: reporting and boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| First stage (regular contractions to before delivery of the fetus) | Contraction interval narrows from 10–15 min gradually to 2–3 min; the mother gradually cannot walk or talk through contractions; the urge to defecate appears late in the stage | First birth 12–18 h; subsequent births 6–10 h | First birth >24 h with no progress (contractions not strengthening, urge to defecate not appearing) = prolonged labor: early waters breaking or abnormal fetal position possible; report to the level-3 birth attendant and the medic; this route has no surgical means — what remains is position (alternating left and right side-lying), fluids, rest, **truthful telling** |
| Second stage (strong urge to defecate to delivery of the fetus) | The mother bears down voluntarily; **follow the mother's pushing rhythm: she pushes when the contraction comes, rests in the interval**; the attendant does not shout counts and does not hurry her | First birth ≤2 h; subsequent births ≤1 h | First birth >2 h without progress triggers assessment; >3 h still without progress = boundary: continue support (fluids, switch to a squatting/kneeling position to use gravity) + truthful telling |
| Internal-examination discipline | Internal examination is an infection entry point with a limited information yield | **≤3 per labor, hands washed before and after each**, used only for: confirming labor onset, and assessment when a line is crossed | "Feeling the cervix" is not done routinely |

**Non-intervention principle (no pulling, no pressing, no cutting without indication)**:

| Forbidden action | Reason | Sole exception |
|---|---|---|
| Pulling the fetal head, pulling the shoulders | Third-degree tearing, newborn brachial-plexus injury | None. Shoulder dystocia (shoulders not delivered >1 min after the head) = boundary case: have the mother shift to the all-fours position (gravity helps release the shoulder) and call for help; if the shoulder still does not come, record truthfully |
| Fundal pressure to assist delivery | Uterine rupture, placental abruption | **Forbidden throughout delivery.** Fundal pressure appears only in the 48.4.1 postpartum-hemorrhage management; it is a bleeding-control action, not a delivery-assist action |
| Routine episiotomy | Cutting without anesthesia + cutting without indication raises infection and tearing | Not done. Perineal tears are managed by the Chapter 44 suturing card: first-degree (skin and mucosa) cleaned and sutured; second degree and above recorded truthfully, cleaned and sutured as far as possible |
| Digging out / pulling the placenta (third stage) | Uterine inversion, massive hemorrhage | With no bleeding, wait (48.4.2); the boundary procedure applies only under the dual condition "massive hemorrhage + retention" (48.4.2) |

One sentence of historical lesson: **excessive intervention is more dangerous than waiting.** Waiting is reversible; a pull that ruins is not.

### 48.3.3 Cord Management

1. **Delayed tying**: after the whole body is delivered and the infant is dried and placed on the mother's chest, wait until pulsation of the cord stops (about 1–3 min, empirical) before tying — let the placental blood flow back to the infant.
2. **Two ties**: with boiled thread, place the first tie 10–15 mm from the cord root (the abdominal-wall skin), a double loop pulled tight (standard ◎: the thread sinks about 1 mm into the cord jelly without slipping — practice method: tie 20 double-loop knots on water-soaked hemp rope; holding 2 kg without slipping is a pass; misjudgment compensation: after tying, watch the stump 5 min for no oozing); the second tie sits 10 mm outside the first.
3. **Cut**: cut with boiled scissors 5 mm outside the second tie; the cut end shows no oozing and no active bleeding.
4. Cord around the neck: if loose, slip it down over the shoulders; if tight (skin color darkening after the head is delivered, no progress of pulsation), the level-3 birth attendant cuts between the two ties and delivers immediately — this is a boundary procedure, with two-person verification and record.

### 48.3.4 The Three Immediate Steps for the Newborn (Warmth First)

The order is fixed, completed within 30–60 s: **① dry (wet cloth over head and body; the wet cloth is replaced by a dry one immediately — a wet wrap is an ice pack); ② onto the mother's chest (skin to skin, a dry cloth over the infant's back); ③ swaddle on**. Wipe mucus from mouth and nose gently with a dry cloth corner or a cloth-wrapped finger; **holding the infant upside down and slapping is forbidden** (a historical error). A strong cry = breathing established. **No cry or weak breathing: if 30 s of drying and stimulation fails, start positive-pressure ventilation — mouth-to-mouth-and-nose (the rescuer's mouth covers both the infant's mouth and nose at once, shallow fast breaths 30–40 breaths/min; chest rise and fall means effective); if no pulse is felt or heart rate <60 beats/min → add chest compressions, ventilation:compressions = 3:1 (30 ventilations and 90 compressions per minute); keep warming and calling for help throughout** (the detailed Chapter 44 cardiopulmonary-resuscitation entry applies). Record continuously. **Warming is the first life-saving action and outranks any examination**: a chilled newborn will not cry and will not suck, then fades — it dies quietly, a typical silent failure as this chapter defines it (failure-mode table, row 2).

Substitutions and losses: unboiled washed cloths (infection risk raised one grade, recorded on file).

Calibration before use: practise the double-loop tie 20 times on wet hemp cord—2 kg hung without slipping passes; cross-check the scale against the 500 g weight; test the warm room against the five verification criteria.
3. **Sequence and control points**: **Do first, in this order**: ① complete the four prenatal preparations a month ahead (Section 48.2.3)—wash hands and nails (nails not past the fingertips, nail folds scrubbed with soap), put scissors, thread, and cloths into the pot the moment labor starts and boil ≥15 min, lay the two underpad layers, warm the room half a day ahead; 4. **Empirical parameters and calibration**: first stage 12–18 h nulliparous, 6–10 h multiparous; second stage ≤2 h nulliparous, ≤1 h multiparous, >2 h triggers assessment and >3 h is the boundary; internal exams ≤3; cord ties at 10–15 mm and 10 mm outside, cutting delayed 1–3 min; the three immediate steps ≤60 s. Calibration: record case by case on the birth record sheet and summarize this settlement's own values quarterly into the ledger (Chapter 43); back-check the cord rule against the stump-oozing rate, and accept the tie by a 2 kg hang.
5. **Observations to record**: time of membrane rupture and character of the fluid (clear / yellow-green), contraction progress, number and times of internal exams, time of birth, cry, cord-cutting time, placenta-delivery time, blood loss (cloth weighing), fundal height and firmness, and the times at which the three cleans and the three immediate steps were done—each in its own column of the birth record sheet.
6. **Pass / rework / scrap criteria**: pass = the three cleans performed + no improper intervention + newborn dried and on the chest within ≤60 s + no stump oozing; rework = a tie slips and oozes (re-tie and record); a first-degree perineal tear is cleaned and sutured (per Card 44-02 and the Chapter 44 suture procedure); scrap (management failure) = violating the non-intervention principle (pulling the head, pressing the fundus, cutting without indication)—recorded as a procedure violation whatever the outcome, and taken into review.
7. **Common failures and diagnosis order** (most frequent first): ① postpartum fever → back-trace the three cleans for the breakpoint (handwashing count and timing? number of internal exams? where the instruments were set down?) → then inspect the perineal wound (Section 44.9.2); ② newborn not crying → first check whether drying and warming were completed → open the airway → stimulate 30 s more → positive-pressure ventilation; ③ bleeding from uterine atony → run the Card 48-02 order; ④ stump oozing → check the number and position of the ties (10–15 mm) → re-tie; ⑤ omphalitis (a red streak running out from the cord root, redness, swelling, pus) → manage as infection per Chapter 44 and report.
8. **L1 / L2 / L3 evidence**: L1 = walk the whole card on a model and state every criterion; L2 = ≥10 qualified records under supervision + three-cleans assessment with no violation; L3 = continuous independent delivery records + one apprentice brought through L2 + the settlement goes two seasons with no puerperal-fever cluster (Chapter 47 scope).

## 48.4 Postpartum: Hemorrhage, Infection, and Breast Milk

### 48.4.1 Postpartum Hemorrhage: Recognition and Management

Postpartum hemorrhage is the number-one killer within maternal death. **Recognition relies not on estimation but on weighing**: after the birth, all blood-soaked cloths (buttock sheets and perineal pads included) are weighed; the weight gain in grams ≈ the blood loss in milliliters (1 g ≈ 1 mL, a physical approximation).

**Decision lines (empirical, re-verified case by case)**: blood loss ≥500 mL within 2 h after delivery; or bleeding of any amount accompanied by signs of shock — pale face, resting pulse >110 beats/min, cold clammy skin, a feeling of fainting. **If either of the two holds, Card 44-02 starts immediately.**

Management sequence (four steps):

| Step | Action | Criteria / key points |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Call a second person + record the time + take the pulse | From decision to start of massage ≤60 s (drill pass line) |
| 2 | **Uterine massage**: first confirm the bladder is empty (the mother should urinate once within 2 h postpartum; if she cannot urinate on her own, induce by pouring warm water over the perineum); one hand steadies the lower uterine segment above the pubic bone, the other feels the fundus above the navel; knead and massage in circles until the uterine body contracts and hardens | ◎ Hardness reading: the uterine body turns from "soft as a lip" to "hard as the tip of the nose," and pressure is maintained until it no longer quickly softens after massage stops. Practice method: ≥10 rounds each on the model (a cloth bag stuffed with chaff and tied shut) and on normal postpartum cases (light touch when there is no bleeding, to learn the feel); misjudgment compensation: use "cloth weighing shows no further increase within 15 min after massage" as the objective line |
| 3 | Infant suckling at the breast (start feeding as early as the infant's condition allows) | Suckling promotes uterine contraction as a physiological mechanism; after each feed, feel the fundus again for firmness |
| 4 | **Abdominal compression method (temporary)**: if the bleeding does not stop, a second person places the flat of a fist on the midline just above the navel, slightly to the left (over the abdominal aorta), and presses steadily and deeply toward the spine | Reduces the blood returning from the lower body and buys time for the massage to take effect; record the start and stop times; withdraw the pressure gradually once the massage takes effect and the uterine body is hard |

**Boundary (written as it is)**: if, after massage + emptied bladder + suckling + abdominal compression, the uterine body still cannot be kept hard within 2 h, the total blood loss exceeds 1000 mL, or the shock signs worsen — this route has no surgery, no uterotonic drugs, no transfusion. What remains: continued massage and abdominal compression, lying flat with the legs raised, warmth, small frequent oral rehydration (3 g salt + 20–30 g sugar per L), attendance, item-by-item recording, and truthful telling. **Exploring the uterine cavity by hand "to do something" is forbidden** (infection and trauma; the death rate rises instead), unless the dual conditions of the 48.4.2 boundary procedure are met.

### 48.4.2 Retained Placenta: Observation Time Limits and Boundary

| Situation | Time limit (empirical) | Management |
|---|---|---|
| No bleeding after the fetus is delivered | Wait ≤30 min: do not pull the cord, do not knead or squeeze; place the infant on the mother's chest to nurse (suckling itself promotes separation) | 30 min and not separated but no bleeding: continue observing to 2 h, recording the fundus and blood loss every 15 min; most separate on their own (separation signs: the cord lengthens by itself, the fundus rises and hardens, a small gush of blood from the vagina) |
| Still not separated at 2 h, no bleeding | **Keep her under observation at the delivery place ≥24 h (she may not go home)**: weigh the perineal pad every 1–2 h, check fundal height and pulse; bleeding >100 mL/h, pulse persistently >110, or a rising fundus → manage per Card 44-02 immediately; not separated at 24 h → the manual-removal boundary or truthful referral |
| **Massive hemorrhage + retained placenta (dual condition)** | Last resort | Manual removal: by a level-3 birth attendant only, two-person verification, three-cleans-level protection throughout (washing to the elbows + alcohol), stopped immediately if it does not succeed in one attempt. This procedure carries extremely high infection and uterine-injury risk; **tell the death rate truthfully before doing it**, and record item by item |

### 48.4.3 Puerperal Fever: Recognition and Back-Tracing

Decision: **from 24 h after delivery, two temperature readings ≥38 °C (Chapter 27 thermometer) at least 4 h apart**, accompanied by the lochia (postpartum vaginal discharge) turning foul-smelling, or lower-abdominal pain or tenderness — that is puerperal infection. Four things within the boundary: the semi-recumbent position (helps the lochia drain out fully; **no method of "plugging" the lochia is used**), perineal cleanliness (rinsing with cooled boiled clean water, a daily routine), oral rehydration and nutritional support, and daily records of temperature and lochia character. **No antibiotics (master exclusion)**: fever persisting >3 days, or chills and listlessness appearing, means signs of infection entering the blood — tell truthfully, attend, record the outcome.

**Every case of puerperal fever is an audit of the 48.3 cleanliness procedure**: the birth attendant back-traces the cleanliness breakpoints per Card 44-01 (number and timing of handwashes, number of internal exams, boiling and placement of instruments, the delivery room and pad cloths, postpartum perineal care), the breakpoint conclusion goes into the case file (Chapter 47), and at least one procedure change must be landed. **The root of puerperal fever is almost always contamination at the delivery; prevention lives in 48.3, not in this section's management.**

### 48.4.4 The Breastfeeding System

| Item | System | Criteria and notes |
|---|---|---|
| Colostrum | **Not discarded**: all of the yellow thick milk of the first 3 days postpartum is fed to the infant | A small colostrum volume is normal; "little" does not mean "no milk"; discarding colostrum while waiting for "real milk" is a common historical error |
| On demand | Feed as soon as the infant shows rooting cues (turning the head, opening the mouth, sucking on hands); upper limit of interval 2–3 h | ≥8 feeds per 24 h (empirical); feed through the night too (night suckling matters for maintaining supply) |
| Exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months | No water added, no rice congee added, no substitute foods of any kind (Chapter 10 grain does not enter the infant's mouth) | Local feasibility premise: the nursing mother's ration is increased about 25% per the Chapter 37 balancing (48.2.1 table); the infant weight-gain table staying on line (48.5.3) is the objective evidence of sufficient milk |
| Feeding hygiene | The mother washes both hands and the nipples with soap daily (once before feeding) | Do not apply alcohol or soap to the nipples and feed without rinsing them clean |

### 48.4.5 The Substitute-Milk Boundary: Second-Best, Not Equivalent

Substitute milk starts only when there is no breast milk (mother dead, seriously ill, or supply persistently below the line with the milk-promoting foods exhausted). **State the gap truthfully: in the historical records, infants without breast milk died within the first year at several times the rate of breastfed infants — beyond the difference in milk species, the bigger killer was contamination in preparation.**

| Age | Formula (mixed fresh and boiled before every feed) | Amount per feed and frequency (empirical) |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 month | 2 parts animal milk + 1 part rice cream (the top layer of thick rice congee) | Start at 10–20 mL, every 2–3 h, adjusted by the weight-gain table |
| 1–3 months | 3 parts animal milk + 1 part rice cream | 30–90 mL, every 3 h |
| From 3 months | Transition gradually to whole animal milk | 90–150 mL, every 3–4 h |

Discipline: animal milk and rice cream must both be boiled (Chapter 6); utensils (horn spoon, cloth strainer, earthenware bowl) are boiled after every use; **weekly weighing of substitute-fed infants must not be skipped** — if weight gain misses the line, re-check the formula and the hygiene (failure-mode table). Finding a wet nurse (shared nursing) always takes priority over any formula; arrange it through the register.

[Card 48-02 Postpartum-Hemorrhage Management Card] (eight fields)

1. **Input materials**: scale (Chapter 3, for weighing cloths, 1 g ≈ 1 mL); boiled cloths and perineal pads on hand ≥6; rehydration salt and sugar (mixed fresh: 3 g salt + 20–30 g sugar per L); a blank "hemorrhage" column on the birth record sheet. Substitutions and losses: without a scale, estimate from the soaked area of the pad cloths and mark "estimated" (±30% conversion error; trend use only).
2. **Tools required**: water clock (times and the 15 min cycle); pulse taking (resting-pulse comparison); massage practice model (a chaff-packed cloth bag); warm-water washing kit (to induce urination). Calibration before use: practise the massage ≥10 times each on the model and on a normal postpartum case; take your own resting pulse first as the comparison value.
3. **Sequence and control points**: **Do first, in this order**: ① **weigh first, then decide**—blood loss ≥500 mL within 2 h of birth, or any bleeding with signs of shock (pale face, resting pulse >110 beats/min, cold clammy skin, feeling of fainting); either one starts the card; ② within **60 s**: call a second person + record the time + take the pulse; ③ empty the bladder (if she cannot, induce it with warm water poured over the perineum)—**check the bladder before massaging**; ④ uterine massage: one hand steadies the lower segment above the pubis, the other finds the fundus above the umbilicus, circular kneading until the uterine body is "hard as the tip of the nose" and does not soften again quickly when the hand stops; ⑤ put the infant to the breast (as soon as her condition allows); ⑥ bleeding not stopping → a second person presses a fist on the midline above the umbilicus, slightly left, straight down toward the spine (abdominal compression), recording start and stop, easing off as the uterus hardens; ⑦ **2 h of observation: weigh every 15 min + re-check fundal firmness + pulse**; ⑧ at the boundary: lie flat with legs raised, keep warm, small frequent oral fluids, continuous massage with someone attending, item-by-item records, truthful telling. **Never do**: explore the uterine cavity by hand (infection and trauma; mortality rises), except under the two conditions of Section 48.4.2 (massive bleeding + retained placenta; level-3 attendant only, two-person verification, stop after one attempt); never "plug" the lochia; never give an unvalidated haemostatic (Chapter 46 scope); never stop the weighing and recording "to do something." **Stop and hand over** (no surgery, no oxytocics, no transfusion on this route—stop the procedure and refer): if after massage + bladder emptying + suckling + compression the uterus still cannot be held hard within 2 h, or total loss >1000 mL, or shock signs worsen—stop at once, move to the boundary procedure, hand over to the medic / refer the same day, and record and tell the truth throughout.
4. **Empirical parameters and calibration**: decision line ≥500 mL within 2 h, shock pulse >110 beats/min; re-check cycle 15 min; massage effect line = no further weight gain on the cloths in the 15 min after the hand stops; boundary line >1000 mL. Calibration: record blood loss, time to massage effect, and outcome case by case; re-verify quarterly, revise, and transcribe to the ledger (Chapter 43).
5. **Observations to record**: start time, whether the bladder was emptied, time to massage effect (decision to sustained hardening), start and stop of abdominal compression, cumulative loss (summed from each weighing), pulse, fundal firmness, outcome—entered in the "hemorrhage" column of the birth record sheet.
6. **Pass / rework / scrap criteria**: pass = massage begun within 60 s of the decision + uterus held hard + no further bleeding within 2 h + complete records; rework = softening and bleeding again after massage (repeat the massage sequence and check whether the bladder has refilled); scrap (management failure) = uterine exploration or another prohibited act performed after the boundary line—recorded as a procedure violation; a death after reaching the boundary goes to the mandatory maternal-death review (Section 48.8.3).
7. **Common failures and diagnosis order** (most frequent first): ① massage ineffective → first check for a full bladder (the commonest reason massage fails); ② uterine atony from multiple birth or overlong labor → flag as high risk prenatally; ③ continuous bleeding from a birth-canal laceration → inspect perineum and cervix, press and suture the tear; ④ retained placenta → with no bleeding wait up to 2 h; only the "massive bleeding + retention" pair justifies the boundary procedure; ⑤ late bleeding (any massive loss from 24 h to 6 weeks postpartum) → manage by this same card + report (possible retained tissue, boundary declaration).
8. **L1 / L2 / L3 evidence**: L1 = recite the four steps + demonstrate the massage on the model; L2 = timed drill (decision to massage ≤60 s) passing 3 times + at least 1 real management record judged qualified; L3 = no "massaged before emptying the bladder" type of omission in the settlement's continuous records, and the maternal-death review executed case by case.

## 48.5 Infants: Warmth, the Cord Stump, the Weight-Gain Dashboard, and Supplementary Food

### 48.5.1 Warmth and Swaddling

A newborn's head is large, its body surface area relatively great, and its subcutaneous fat thin; it loses heat several times faster than an adult. Three rules of the warmth system:

| Item | Method | Criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Room temperature | Same room as the mother (not left alone in a separate room); sleep spot away from door and window gaps and from drafts | An adult sitting still in a single layer of clothing does not feel cold (same as verification item 5) |
| Swaddling | Wrap in a swaddle (Chapter 15 cloth); snugness such that **a flat adult palm can slide in at the chest**; only mouth and nose left outside | Too tight hinders breathing and hip development; too loose does not keep warm |
| Temperature rounds | ◎ Before and after every feed, test the infant's **nape** with the back of an adult's hand (not the hands and feet — cool hands and feet are normal) | Warm = right; cool = add a layer and re-test after 30 min; damp with sweat = remove a layer. Practice method: mother and birth attendant each test 20 times to align their readings; misjudgment compensation: supplement with an armpit reading on the Chapter 27 thermometer, ≥36.5 °C as the objective line |

Recognizing hypothermia (silent failure): cool skin, not feeding, not crying, limbs limp — **for any infant that "suddenly turns quiet and good," test the nape first**.

### 48.5.2 Umbilical Stump Care

| Item | Method | Criteria and abnormal findings |
|---|---|---|
| Routine | Stump left exposed, kept dry and clean; no bandaging, no powders or ointments of any kind applied; the diaper (cloth pad) folded below the stump to keep it from soaking | Dries and blackens naturally; falls off in 3–14 days (empirical) |
| Daily observation | Birth attendant visits and family members check the stump base daily | A red streak running outward from the cord root, or redness, swelling, and pus at the base = omphalitis → manage as infection per Chapter 44 + report; oozing blood = re-tie (instrument standard of Card 48-02) |
| Boundary | Omphalitis with fever, refusal to feed, lethargy | Risk of infection entering the blood (including tetanus, 44.9.4 — a contaminated cord is the number-one entry point for newborn tetanus; **tetanus has no solution in this route; dryness and cleanliness are the only line of defense**); tell truthfully, attend and record |

### 48.5.3 Weight Gain: The Health Dashboard

With no lab work and no instruments, **weight gain is the only continuously measurable gauge of infant health**. Tools: the Chapter 3 scale (steelyard or balance) + the Chapter 15 cloth sling + the Chapter 7 beam. Weigh weekly under the same conditions: before a feed, undressed, on the same scale (cross-checked against the 500 g standard weight each time; a reading difference ≤50 g means usable).

| Time point / age | Criterion (empirical, revised on re-verification) | Meaning of missing the line and management |
|---|---|---|
| Birth weight | 2.5–4.0 kg | <2.5 kg = high risk: watch warming and feeding closely (prematurity or intrauterine malnutrition); enter in the high-risk register |
| Days 7–10 | Back up to birth weight (physiological loss ≤10%) | Not recovered by day 10: check sucking strength and feeding frequency; milk-promoting foods + feeds ≥10 per day |
| 0–3 months | Weekly gain ≥150 g (about ≥600 g per month) | Two consecutive weeks off the line = insufficient breast milk or the infant is ill (mouth, infection); check 48.4.4 and the Chapter 47 triage the same day |
| 3–6 months | Weekly gain ≥100 g | Same as above |
| 6–12 months | Weekly gain ≥50 g | Missing the line is mostly related to supplementary food or repeated diarrhea (48.5.4 / 48.5.5) |

Calibration: each line is re-verified quarterly against this settlement's case-by-case records and transcribed into the ledger; no number on the table may be changed by impression.

### 48.5.4 Supplementary-Food Timing

| Item | System | Criteria and discipline |
|---|---|---|
| Starting point | A full 6 months (Chapter 10 calendar), able to sit with support, and a spoon entering the mouth is not pushed out by the tongue | All three conditions met before supplementary food starts; nothing added before 5 months (digestion and infection risk) |
| First-choice foods | **Iron-rich first**: liver paste, meat paste, bean paste; then egg yolk, thick porridge | An infant's iron stores run out at 6 months, and grain porridge cannot carry on — this is the typical failure of the "porridge-only" feeding style (48.6.3) |
| New-food discipline | Add one food at a time; watch 3 consecutive days with no vomiting, rash, or diarrhea before adding the next | Record the start date and the reaction for each (registered in Chapter 47 case style) |
| Amount | Start at 1–2 spoons (10–20 g), doubling every 3–5 days; 2 meals/day at 9 months, 3 meals/day at 12 months (empirical) | Verified against the weight-gain table; amounts are not increased just because "he likes it" |
| Hygiene | Feeding utensils boiled at every use; food made fresh and eaten fresh, never kept overnight | — |

### 48.5.5 Infants With Diarrhea, and the Substitute Defenses for the Vaccine Exclusion

**Diarrhea**: infants dehydrate far faster than adults (small body weight, fast turnover). Management follows Chapter 47 47.3.3 entirely: dehydration grading + oral rehydration (1 L clean water + 3 g salt + 20–30 g sugar, weighed and mixed fresh, small amounts often — 10–20 mL per feed for an infant, one spoonful every 5–10 min); **continue breastfeeding; no fasting and no withholding fluids**. Criteria for moving up to the Chapter 44 emergency level: sunken eyes, crying without tears, sunken fontanelle, clearly reduced urine (no urine for 12 h), listlessness. Every case of diarrhea is entered in the case register (Chapter 47) — seasonal clustering of infant diarrhea is a direct clue to water-source contamination (Chapter 45).

**Truthful declaration on vaccine exclusion and the substitute defenses**: for measles, smallpox, whooping cough, and other epidemic diseases, this route has **no vaccines** (master exclusion; variolation and other early inoculation are not provided as a route — they can themselves cause epidemics and death, and there is no assay system). Only three substitute defenses exist: **① the isolation system** (Chapter 45: find and isolate patients early, suspend gatherings during epidemics, visit and register affected households); **② hygiene engineering** (Chapter 45 drinking water, feces management, vector control — the main line of defense against intestinal epidemics); **③ nutrition and weight-gain monitoring** (48.6; malnourished children die of these diseases at multiples of the usual rate — historical records). All three defenses stand before the patient falls ill; this is the endpoint of this chapter's "prevention over cure" posture.

## 48.6 The Three Major Deficiency Diseases: "Enough Grain, Yet People Still Fall Ill"

The Chapter 10 grain equivalent guarantees only calories. Calories met yet people ill — that is this section's subject. **The common trait of these three diseases is that they ruin health slowly: the patient and the caregiver long fail to see it as "disease," and by the time typical symptoms appear it is already late-stage.** The body of this section is therefore not treatment: the reserve structure (Chapter 10) and the registration system (Chapter 47) stand in front, with the recognition tables behind.

### 48.6.1 Scurvy (Vitamin C Deficiency)

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| Recognition | Gums red and swollen, bleeding spontaneously (brushing the teeth or biting a hard loaf draws blood); skin petechiae (mostly on the shins, at pressure sites); fatigue; wounds ulcerating and slow to heal; old scars splitting open; **peaks in late winter and early spring** (the deepest point of the fresh-vegetable gap) |
| Prevention | Land the three tiers of the Chapter 10 reserve structure: **dried fruits-and-vegetables, pickled vegetables (which keep part of the vitamin C), and beans** each occupying one tier; issue **sprouted beans / sprouts** once every 7–10 days in winter (vitamin C content rises markedly during bean sprouting, Chapter 10 sprouting method); keep the fresh-tuber cellar stocked (Chapter 10) |
| Management | Therapeutic large doses of fresh food: ≥300 g of fresh vegetables or an equivalent amount of sprouts + pickled vegetables daily (split across 2–3 meals, empirical), while **registering symptom reversal case by case**: start date, daily food source, on which day the gum bleeding eased, on which day it essentially disappeared (historical records mostly show relief within days to two weeks; this settlement's register is the authority). Write no "miracle cure"; write only the register |
| Boundary | Those already bedridden, with widespread old bleeding points, or with deep ulcerated sores recover slowly; tell truthfully; register the outcome. A late-winter outbreak = the reserve structure lacks the vegetable tier, triggering a Chapter 10 reserve-tier back-check and the row-3 handling of the failure-mode table |

### 48.6.2 Iodine Deficiency (Goiter)

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| Recognition | Swelling of the thyroid region at the front of the neck (moving up and down with the larynx when swallowing is the distinguishing point); endemic — several people in the same settlement with the same neck swelling means endemic iodine deficiency; in severely deficient areas children lag in physical and mental development, with high rates of deaf-mutism and cretinism (found by register comparison) |
| Prevention | The local route is given by geography: coast / near a lake — sea salt (Chapter 23), kelp, shellfish, and fish ≥1–2 times a week; inland salt-lake line — brine and salt from the salt lake; purely inland with no marine source of any kind — **declare truthfully that there is no reliable supplementation route**. **Drinking iodine tincture is strictly forbidden** (an external-use disinfectant; the dose cannot be controlled and it can kill). When selecting or relocating a site, list the iodine source as an assessment item (Chapters 50–53 environment editions) |
| Management | Where an iodine source exists, after intensifying food sources register the swelling changes case by case (a matter of months' scale; no promise of regression); swelling compressing breathing or swallowing = beyond the boundary, state truthfully |
| Boundary | In iodine-deficient areas the registration system matters more than management: Chapter 47 surveys the goiter rate (a roster entry every half year), marked down to the household, for citation in settlement site-selection, salt-route, and marriage-radius decisions |

### 48.6.3 Anemia (Mostly Iron Deficiency)

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| Recognition | Paleness of the palpebral conjunctiva (pull down the lower eyelid), nail beds, and lips — **compared against the caregiver's own same sites under the same light** (◎ reading practice: healthy people examine one another 20 times to unify the readings; misjudgment compensation: Chapter 27, "refill time after pressing the nail bed >2 s" as an objective supporting sign); fatigue, falling work endurance, brittle nails that curve upward; in children, pica (eating earth, ash) as a clue |
| Prevention | The iron-source table (48.2.1): animal blood, liver, red meat, beans; **empirical practice: serve iron-rich foods in the same meal as fresh vegetables / pickled vegetables rich in vitamin C** (register same-meal or not, case by case, for comparison); iron-pot cooking (ironware, Chapters 18–19) as an additional item. **Parasites are also a source of anemia**: hookworm enters through the skin (barefoot in the fields) and through children's hand-to-mouth and soil-eating — feces management and the toilet system (Chapter 45), shoes in the fields, soap handwashing before meals |
| Management | After food-source intensification, re-check the observation items (complexion, endurance, nails) at 4–6 weeks, registering case by case; **repeated anemia in a child = run the parasite line**: ask about a history of barefoot field work, examine the stool (worms visible to the eye; follow-up clues registered per Chapter 47), and once improvement occurs the feces-management breakpoint must be closed, or it recurs |
| Boundary | Severe anemia in pregnancy (breathlessness at rest, palpitations, leg swelling) = high-risk pregnancy register: poor tolerance of delivery and a high death rate from postpartum hemorrhage — **no transfusion route; tell truthfully**, with intensified food sources prenatally as the focus |

### 48.6.4 Appendix Table: Protein-Energy Deficiency and Vitamin A Night Blindness

| Condition | Recognition | Management | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Protein-energy deficiency — edematous type | Weight not clearly reduced, even looking puffy, but pitting dents on the lower legs and face when pressed, listlessness, hair dry and brittle and pulling out easily | **Add protein gradually**: start at 1/4 amount, up one step every 3–5 days (a sudden large amount → diarrhea and death, a historical lesson); eggs, beans, milk, meat paste | Concurrent infection is the main cause of death; watch it together under the Chapter 47 triage |
| Protein-energy deficiency — wasting type | Skin and bones, weight fallen below the lower line of the weight-gain table, subcutaneous fat gone (a fold cannot be pinched up on the abdominal wall) | Same gradual method + calories first (Chapter 10 grain equivalent) | — |
| Vitamin A night blindness | Cannot see at dusk or in the dark, walks into objects; dry eyes | Liver, egg yolk, dark-leaved vegetables (Chapter 10); liver ≥2 times a week; register the resolution of night blindness case by case | Progression to light intolerance with eyes held shut, or corneal clouding = risk of blindness; tell truthfully |

[Card 48-03 Deficiency-Disease Recognition and Intervention-Dosing Card] (eight fields)

1. **Input materials**: reserve-tier inventory (one tier each of dried fruits-and-vegetables, pickled vegetables, beans; Chapter 10); scale (Chapters 3/22, for weighing salt, sugar, and food sources); infant hanging scale (500 g check weight); register book (paper and ink, Chapter 41).

3. **Operation sequence and control points**: ① verify the three reserve tiers are in store before the season (completed before winter); **② late-winter/early-spring settlement-wide survey (the tail of the fresh-vegetable gap): register all four items — gums, petechiae, neck swelling, palpebral conjunctiva — person by person**; ③ for positives, start food-source intervention per the 48.6 tables and register symptom reversal; **④ monthly two-line check of infant and pregnant-mother weight gain / complexion**; ⑤ for repeated anemia, run the parasite line (barefoot history + feces-management breakpoint); ⑥ half-yearly goiter-rate summary (Chapter 47 scope).
4. **Empirical parameters and field calibration**: the scurvy treatment dose ≥300 g/day of fresh food, seafood 1–2 times a week, liver ≥2 times a week, and the 4–6 week intervention observation window are all empirical values; re-verify and revise against the timescales of symptom change in the case-by-case registration; transcribe into the ledger.
5. **Process observation record items**: survey date, the four-item positive findings per case, intervention food source and amount, re-check dates and changes, outcome — entered in the deficiency-disease register (bound quarterly into the Chapter 47 case system).
6. **Pass / rework / scrap criteria**: pass = survey coverage ≥95% (against the roster headcount) + all positives filed + registration complete across two seasons. Rework = households missed (re-survey and record the reason). Scrap = thinking to check the reserve tiers only after a late-winter outbreak has already happened — record as a failure case, triggering revision of the Chapter 10 reserve structure.
7. **Common failures and troubleshooting order**: anemia intervention with no improvement at 4–6 weeks → first check whether the food source was actually delivered (ledger) → check same-meal vitamin C → check the parasite line (barefoot / feces management) → if pregnant, escalate as high risk. Scurvy register's "fresh food" not matching reality → check whether sprouts were actually issued on the 7–10 day rotation. Goiter rate not falling → check whether the salt route actually delivers sea salt (accounts).
8. **L1/L2/L3 evidence**: L1 = can state the recognition criteria of all three major diseases from the four-column tables + completes one survey form-filling; L2 = independently completes one late-winter survey + ≥3 intervention registrations complete; L3 = two consecutive years of survey records on file + pre-season reserve-tier checks on record + no late-winter scurvy outbreak in the settlement, with the anemia-intervention improvement rate entered in the ledger.

## 48.7 Menstruation, Contraception, and the Limited Means of Reproductive Health

### 48.7.1 Truthful Registration: This Route Has No Reliable Contraception

| Method | Effect and limitations (truthful) | Institutional meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Calendar method (record the periods, avoid intercourse on the fertile days) | Requires recording the start day of every period (entered in the Chapter 47 roster); **even strictly followed, unintended pregnancy within a year remains common (historical records show persistently high failure rates)** — irregular cycles (worse under poor nutrition and heavy labor) weaken reliability further | Another value of registering cycles: when the period fails to come, pregnancy versus illness can be distinguished one step earlier |

**Population planning's proper place is not this section but Chapter 37**: birth spacing (a child every two years or every three years) is back-derived from the carrying-capacity estimate book (Chapter 37, N = min upper bound ÷ k), approximated as well as possible by three imperfect tools — the late-marriage age line, the breastfeeding system, and the calendar method. **What cannot be approximated is admitted truthfully, not covered by claiming there is a way.** If a settlement passes the 80% carrying-capacity warning line (Chapter 37), what tightens is rationing, land-clearing, and relocation decisions — not shifting the responsibility onto contraception technology that cannot deliver.

### 48.8.3 Per-Birth Records and the Mandatory Maternal-Death Review

One sheet per birth, archived within 24 h, format continuing the Chapter 47 case card, fixed columns: last menstrual period and expected delivery month / danger-signal registration / duration of each stage of labor / **intervention list (number and times of internal exams, cord-cutting time, massage or not, any pulling, pressing, or cutting — an empty cell means zero)** / blood loss (per weighing) / infant weight, cry, and warming time points / placenta condition / results of the three postpartum visits (within 24 h, day 3, days 7–10) / outcome / dual signatures of birth attendant and verifier.

**Maternal death (mother or infant) mandates a review**, continuing the Chapter 47 47.2.3 no-blame review system: before the end of the month, the birth attendant + medic (or health worker) + one council member + the attending family members go back through the whole register — delays in seeking care, danger signals ignored, execution of the three cleans, the intervention list, the timeliness of hemorrhage management; **at least one procedure change must be produced** (which table to change, which criterion, who executes), archived in Chapter 43. The purpose of the review is not to find someone to blame; it is to press the risk of the next case down.

## Failure Modes and Diagnosis

Ordered by most common and hardest to detect. This chapter's silent failures cluster in three places: puerperal fever (contamination first, fever later), newborn hypothermia (dies quietly), and late-winter scurvy (strikes while the granary is full).

| Symptom | Candidate causes | Verification method | Management | Prevention |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fever after 24 h postpartum + foul-smelling lochia | Puerperal fever — delivery contamination (any breakpoint among handwashing, internal exams, instruments, pad cloths) | **Back-trace the three cleans item by item per Card 44-01** + inspect the perineal wound | Semi-recumbent drainage, perineal cleaning, rehydration, records; no antibiotics — tell truthfully | Three-cleans system; internal exams ≤3; instruments boiled |
| Infant "suddenly turns quiet": not feeding, not crying, cool skin, limp limbs | Newborn hypothermia | Back of the hand on the nape + armpit temperature <36.5 °C | **Immediate skin-to-skin rewarming + dry swaddle + warm room**; fire-baking and hot-water-bottle rapid heat forbidden; re-test at 30 min | Three immediate steps ≤60 s; swaddle and room-temperature criteria |
| Multiple people with bleeding gums, petechiae, fatigue in late winter / early spring | Scurvy outbreak = **the reserve structure lacks the vegetable tier** (Chapter 10 three tiers not landed) | Check the reserve-tier inventory (dried fruits-and-vegetables / pickled vegetables / beans) + settlement survey | Therapeutic fresh food + symptom-reversal register; **triggers revision of the Chapter 10 reserve structure** | Pre-winter check of the three tiers; sprouts issued on the 7–10 day rotation |
| Child's anemia relapsing, food-source intervention ineffective | Parasites (hookworm) — barefoot field work, feces-management breakpoint | Ask about the barefoot history, examine stool and perianal area, check the Chapter 45 toilet-use account | Continue food sources + close the breakpoint (shoes, feces management, handwashing) | Feces-management system; soap handwashing before meals |
| Infant off the weight-gain line for 2 consecutive weeks | Insufficient breast milk / too few feeds / the infant is ill | Check feed count (≥8 per 24 h?) + mouth inspection + Chapter 51 triage | Milk-promoting foods + frequency ≥10 per day; still off the line → substitute milk per 48.4.5 | Weekly weighing never skipped; nursing-mother ration +25% entered in the ledger |
| Massive bleeding again at 2–6 weeks postpartum | Retained placental tissue or poor uterine involution | Measure by weighing cloths | Manage per Card 44-02 + report (boundary declaration) | Check the placenta's completeness at delivery and record |
| Umbilical stump red, swollen, oozing pus | Omphalitis (care contamination, diaper soaking) | Inspection + direction of the red streak | Infection management (Chapter 44) + report; escalate at once if fever | Stump dry and exposed; nothing applied, nothing wrapped |
| Substitute-fed infant with diarrhea, off the weight line | Preparation contamination (utensils not boiled, milk kept overnight) | Ask about the process + inspect the feeding utensils | Mix fresh and boil each time + oral rehydration (Chapter 47) + records | Boil at every feed; never kept over |

| Goiter rate rising half-year by half-year | Salt route broken (sea salt replaced by local salt) or moved into an iodine-deficient area | Check the salt accounts (Chapter 38) + roster | Restore the sea salt / seafood line; if no source, site assessment (Chapters 50–53) | Half-yearly rate summary; salt-route accounts |

## Safety Boundary

> **Hard gate restated (landing the Part declaration)**: wherever the criteria section of a medical card in this chapter writes in a "beyond the boundary" situation, stop at once; what remains is warming / rehydration / pain relief / attendance / records (see the Part declaration in Chapter 44).

The following items are part of the process steps (Iron Law Seven); not executing them counts as not completing the management:

1. **The three cleans are a non-negotiable precondition**: a delivery performed without handwashing and instrument boiling counts as no delivery; hands must be re-washed before and after every internal examination during labor.
2. **The non-intervention principle outranks good intentions**: pulling, pressing, cutting, or digging out without indication is forbidden across the board; waiting is reversible, injury is not.
3. Warmth first: the newborn is dried and placed on the mother's chest before any examination or weighing; rapid heating is forbidden for a chilled infant.
4. Cesarean section, forceps, and uterine exploration (except under the boundary-procedure conditions) are not done — **declare and record truthfully when there is nowhere left to refer; do not create the illusion of heroic surgery**.
5. No one leaves the mother's side for 2 h postpartum: bleeding weighed and fundus palpated every 15 min; executed as usual after night births.
6. Tetanus has no solution: the dry, clean cord stump is the only line of defense; drinking iodine tincture is strictly forbidden; variolation is not provided as a route.
7. Weekly weighing of substitute-fed infants and boiling at every feed must not be skipped; a severely dehydrated infant moves at once to the Chapter 44 emergency level.
