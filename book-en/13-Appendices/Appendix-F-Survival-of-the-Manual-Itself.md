# Appendix F · Survival of the Manual Itself: Key, Media, and Self-Reproduction

> **What this appendix is for**: Chapter 41 of the body text teaches papermaking and ink-making, Chapter 42 teaches drafting and archiving — those are the capabilities of **after the rebuild**. Across the most dangerous stretch of the gap (ten to a hundred years), this manual itself is nothing but a few volumes of paper, or a web page that will not open. This appendix answers one question: **how the manual itself crosses the gap.** It takes no chapter number, sets no process card, and the body text is unchanged.

Three design constraints, stated up front:

1. Reproduction of any page depends only on this appendix or on early chapters of the body text (the bootstrap of iron rule one);
2. A reader with no modern education can still enter — **the key comes before the writing**;
3. No single carrier is trusted.

## 1. The Rosetta Key: An Entry Point Across Languages and Across Eras

- **Mathematical and graphical skeleton**: every concept is expressed first as "number plus figure" (the unit system of Chapter 3, the symbol system of Chapter 56). The key page is an icon vocabulary list:
  - ① Unit figures: one hand-length, one pace, one bucket-volume, one day-time — each paired with a picture of the physical object; **define the local quantity first, then anchor it**;
  - ② Hazard classification figures: flame, skull, collapse, poison mist — four symbols, used throughout the whole library;
  - ③ Process-card eight-field icons: input / tools / steps / parameters / record / verdict / failure / evidence — eight figures in one column, so that even a person who cannot read recognizes the card's structure.
- **Multilingual parallel text**: the general outline, the life-saving chapters (Chapters 44 and 45), the method chapter (Chapter 25) are laid out in parallel in no fewer than three languages, each paragraph side by side. Language selection: the locally current language plus any one surviving major language. The volume carries a "500-word noun–figure concordance" as the entry page for language learning.
- **Letter and number anchors**: the foot of every page is printed with the three-segment number "volume-chapter-page" (e.g., 0-3-1), and at the same position a notch pattern is pressed in (Braille-grade, touchable) — so that pages surviving water damage or scorching can still be put back in place. **The ability to re-place matters more than completeness**: if a damaged page can find its position, the knowledge is still there.

## 2. The Media Ladder: Expected Life and Cost

| Medium | Expected life (empirical) | Cost | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper (Chapter 41 process, oil-impregnated against damp) | Decades | Low | Working copies, daily circulation |
| Waxed parchment or tanned hide | Hundred-year grade | Medium | Master copy (key pages, iron rules, life-saving chapters) |
| Fired clay tablet (Chapter 11, incised and glaze-filled with ink) | Thousand-year grade; fragile to dropping | Medium | The ten iron rules, key pages, unit tables — "civilizational foundation pieces" |
| Copper plate etching (extension of the Chapter 26 acid etching) | Thousand-year grade | High | Off-site redundant master copies of the key pages, one set of three plates in three places |

The life column is entirely empirical: it depends on storage conditions (damp or dry, insects and rodents, fire) more than on the material. There is only one rule: **separate the master copy from the working copies** — the master copy is sealed away (clay jar, wax seal, the Chapter 43 archive system), while working copies circulate and wear; a damaged working copy is recopied from the master copy, and a destroyed master copy is re-engraved from the third copy held off-site.

## 3. The Self-Reprint Loop: The Minimum Tooling to Reproduce the Manual Itself

- **Tooling list** (all pre-industrial): paper (Chapter 41), ink (pine-soot ink cut with tung oil), brush (bristle brush, Chapter 15), copy board (planed wooden board plus the Chapter 42 mirrored reversed-character engraved plate). **Woodblock printing takes precedence over movable type** — movable type needs the Chapter 19 metallurgy and the Chapter 22 precision chain, while woodblock printing any workshop can do;
- **Reprint procedure**: master copy → two people copy separately → sentence-by-sentence collation (differences recorded and adjudicated) → engrave the block → pull three proof sheets for cross-checking → batch printing → signature and seal (version number, copyist, collator);
- **Version discipline** (the Chapter 42 system moved forward): the title page of every volume carries the version line "volume-edition-year-place". When two volumes differ in version, the higher edition governs, and **the old edition is destroyed or demoted to practice paper** — old editions circulating in parallel are the entry point for wrong knowledge to come back to life. The failure mode is very concrete: two editions in parallel, each passing on half, and fifty years later nobody knows which half is right.

## 4. Redundant Deployment: 3×3×3

- **Three master copies, three media, three locations.** Location spacing is judged by "disasters of different origin": different water systems, different slope aspects, different settlements. Each site keeps one page of "repair instructions" (which volume is missing, whom to find, by what procedure to reprint);
- **The handover ceremony**: once every ten years, a "Handover Day" — the new generation counts the volumes in person, recites the ten iron rules in public, the outgoing keeper hands over in public, and the keepers' names are entered in the register (Chapter 43). **A collection without a ceremony is not a collection**: in the history of documents, the overwhelming majority of losses were not from fire but from "nobody knew what this was."

## 5. Reading Priority: What to Rescue First After the Gap

1. **The key pages of this appendix** — lose them and the whole book becomes waste paper;
2. **The life-saving chapters** (Chapters 44 and 45: bleeding control, water purification, wounds, disinfection);
3. **The method chapters** (Chapters 3 and 25: measurement and comparison — the foundation of all knowledge that follows);
4. **Skeleton pages** (Chapter 6 fire, Chapter 10 farming, Chapter 11 pottery);
5. The rest according to local need.

In binding, levels 1–3 are bound together as one "narrow volume" (carryable on the back); the rest are "wide volumes" (library storage). In a disaster the narrow volume goes with the people, while the wide volumes stay where they are and take their chances with the building — these two sentences decide **rescue order**, not collecting preference.

## 6. Acceptance Criteria

- **L1**: the key page passes the "non-literate person test" — someone who has never seen this manual can, from the key page alone, point out the structure of the eight-field card and the meaning of the hazard symbols;
- **L2**: one complete self-reprint is finished (master copy → copy → collate → engrave → print → seal), and the new volume passes sentence-by-sentence comparison against the master copy;
- **L3**: a 3×3×3 deployment is on record, one Handover Day ceremony is fully recorded; one "water-damage rescue" drill is completed (soak one volume, rescue a readable copy per the procedure).
